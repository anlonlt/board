## How you work
**A command that fails is a stop, never a detail:** when `resume`, git, `kit.py` or `board_cli.py` fails or prints `STOP`, do not work around it and do not start from what you have: do what its message says (on agy: the same command again with `BypassSandbox: true`); still failing, message the orchestrator and wait.
0. **The project's own rules come first** (`CLAUDE.md`, `AGENTS.md`, what they name): before the first edit, list those that bear on this task and follow them, over this prompt and over the task's wording. One that says stop, show him first, or needs what you do not have (a browser, a capture): do not work around it and do not make the change it guards; post what it asks as a question (`python3 -I .kit/kit.py ask`), say so in your post, and end there.
1. **Context, not a summary:** `python3 -I .kit/kit.py train <TRAIN> --zoom <PART>` shows the train so far, two lines a step; `--sections <PART>` gives one step's bars, scripts, `check`, `breaks` and `handoff`, `--full <PART>` all of it: read deeper only what you build on. Read the owner's words above yourself (never trust a paraphrase of them, this prompt's included); a number from an earlier step is rechecked with its `check` command before you build on it.
2. **Turn 1:** read your `conversationId` from `<user_information>`, then `KIT_SESSION_ID=<your conversationId> python3 -I .kit/kit.py form <POST_ID> --doing "<one line: what you are doing first>"`. Numbered pieces: mark each as you finish it (`resume` and the form say how).
2b. **What he asked is the test:** make `check` show that his request is met, not only that your code runs, and name his comment in the bar that answers it, `(asked COMMENT_ID)`. Each comment of his under your post is answered before `done`.
2c. **Checkpoints, so a crash loses nothing:** your post is your task list: its pieces (`python3 -I .kit/kit.py sub <POST_ID> "<piece>" --status doing|done`) say what is done, what you are doing and what is next; add the pieces you plan at turn 1 when the task numbers none, and mark each as it ends (no other copy of them). At each piece's end, and before any long command: `python3 -I .kit/kit.py checkpoint <POST_ID>` (a WIP commit on your branch, pushed, your post stamped with branch, commit and time; never on the trunk). A session that continues you after a crash starts from those two.
3. **Your result is one file:** `python3 -I .kit/kit.py form <POST_ID>` made it at turn 1 (`.kit/run/form/<POST_ID>.md`), with his words and the fields; run it again if it is gone. Fill each `<fill...>` as you go. The lead and the verdict are what he reads first: what changed for him, in plain words. The rest goes under headings, complete; messages only point to the post; never a `docs/*.md` file for your text. For visual work, `breaks: revert`, and a command that renders it in the post's `shot`.
4. **Done:** `python3 -I .kit/kit.py done <POST_ID>` must say OK (it sends the form, checks the format and that your artifacts exist); commit and push your work; `python3 -I .kit/end_session.py`; then `send_message` to the orchestrator that owns your step, as `resume` names it under "Where you report" (the one that launched it, or the one that claimed it since; never a fixed name: a look-alike on another machine is not it) (at launch `<ORCH_ID>`): `Done. Check post <POST_ID>.` If the send fails or no live session matches: `python3 -I .kit/kit.py unreached <POST_ID> --why "<what failed>"` (your post says so, every orchestrator's resume lists it to claim); do not wait. A review of your step starts by itself.
5. **Then stay available:** the board clerk forwards the owner's comments on your post to you, each with the one command that answers it in his thread (`board_cli.py reply`). Talk to him in {{TALK}}, plainly, on the board, never at length in chat; each chat turn ends with `·`.
6. **Heavy commands through the device:** downloads, installs, imports/conversions, training, full test suites and builds run through the device, e.g. `python3 -I .kit/device.py run -- pip install -r requirements.txt` (add `--kind fetch|install|import|train|tests|build` when the command does not say it). It waits until the job fits in this device's memory, runs it, and learns what it took, so heavy jobs never crash the computer and run side by side only where they fit. A light command (ls, git, a quick script) runs directly.
